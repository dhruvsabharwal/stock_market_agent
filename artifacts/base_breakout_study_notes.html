Core Philosophy
Why this strategy works and what it's really about

The Core Idea

Stocks move in trends, interrupted by periods of consolidation. The strategy finds stocks that are:

  • ✓
    In a confirmed uptrend (Stage 2)
  • ✓
    Digesting gains in a constructive base (not breaking down)
  • ✓
    Being quietly accumulated by institutions (volume signature)
  • ✓
    Coiling tighter and tighter (VCP contraction)
  • ✓
    About to break out with a specific catalyst or just the exhaustion of selling

The Kell Price Cycle

Every trending stock goes through this lifecycle around its moving averages:

Where we play

We enter at Stage A/B — during the base and at the breakout. We exit when Stage D/E signals appear. We never chase a climax run.

True Relative Strength
The single most important technical indicator in this strategy

What It Is (Not RSI)

Critical Distinction

True RS Line ≠ RSI. The RSI (Relative Strength Index) is an oscillator measuring overbought/oversold. It is NOT used in this strategy. The True RS Line measures price outperformance vs the S&P 500 over time.

The Calculation

// RS Line = ratio of stock price to SPY price, at every point in time
RS_line[t] = Stock_Close[t] / SPY_Close[t]

// RS % change over lookback window
RS_4w = (RS_line[-1] / RS_line[-4] - 1) × 100
RS_13w = (RS_line[-1] / RS_line[-13] - 1) × 100
RS_26w = (RS_line[-1] / RS_line[-26] - 1) × 100
RS_52w = (RS_line[-1] / RS_line[-52] - 1) × 100

// Note: absolute value doesn't matter — only direction and relative change

What the RS Line Tells You

RS Line BehaviourMeaningAction
Rising — at 52-week highInstitutions are accumulating aggressivelyStrong buy candidate
Rising — not at highRecovering outperformanceWatch closely
Flat during baseHolding ground vs marketAcceptable, monitor
Falling during baseInstitutions rotating outAvoid — distribution
At 52w high BEFORE breakoutLEADING RS SIGNALHighest conviction entry

The Leading RS Signal Explained

Leading RS — RS line makes new high while price still consolidates
Intuition

If the RS line is making new highs while the stock is still consolidating and hasn't broken out — institutions are buying the dips. They're more interested in the stock than in the broad market. When the base resolves, they're already positioned and will push it hard.

The VCP — Volatility Contraction Pattern
The coil. The most powerful concept in the entire strategy.

What It Is

A series of progressively smaller pullbacks from left to right inside the base. Each contraction is roughly 40–50% shallower than the prior. This signals seller exhaustion.

VCP — Each swing smaller than the last (30% → 15% → 6%)

The Seller Exhaustion Story — Three Acts

ActWhat HappensWho's Doing ItVolume Signal
Act 1
Shakeout		Big drop from the high. Looks scary.Retail profit-takers, stop-loss triggers, weak handsHeavy — panic selling
Act 2
Accumulation		Stock stabilises. Each pullback is smaller. Institution absorbs every dip.Large institution building position quietly over weeksDrying up — fewer sellers
Act 3
Launch		Almost no sellers left. Any buying = explosive move up.Institution finished + new buyers arrive + stops triggered above pivotSurges on breakout

VCP Maths — How to Measure

// Split the base into thirds
Swing1 = (max(segment1) - min(segment1)) / max(segment1) × 100
Swing2 = (max(segment2) - min(segment2)) / max(segment2) × 100
Swing3 = (max(segment3) - min(segment3)) / max(segment3) × 100

// Pass condition
VCP contracting = Swing3 < Swing2 < Swing1

// Ideal ratio: each ~50% of prior
// e.g. 30% → 15% → 7% = textbook VCP
Spring Metaphor

Think of the VCP as a spring being compressed. Each contraction tightens the spring further. By the time the third contraction forms at near-zero range and volume, the spring is fully compressed. Any buying pressure triggers the release — and the move is proportional to how tightly the spring was coiled.

Volume Analysis
Volume is the X-ray that reveals what institutions are doing

The Two Volume Checks

Check 1 — Accumulation Ratio (weekly)

Accumulation_Ratio = Avg_Volume(up weeks) / Avg_Volume(down weeks)

// Pass: ratio > 1.0 (more vol on green weeks)
// Strong: ratio > 1.3
// Warning: ratio < 0.85 (distribution — institutions are selling into rallies)

Check 2 — Right-Side Volume Dry-Up

Left_Avg = mean(volume, first half of base)
Right_Avg = mean(volume, second half of base)
Vol_Dry = (Right_Avg / Left_Avg - 1) × 100

// Pass: Vol_Dry ≤ −20% (right side at least 20% quieter)
// Strong: ≤ −35%

Volume on the Breakout Day

Volume vs 50-day AverageSignalAction
≥ 2.0× ("elephant tracks")Strong — institutional demand confirmedEnter Entry 2 with confidence
1.5× – 2.0×Acceptable — breakout validEnter Entry 2
1.0× – 1.5×Weak — higher failure riskWait for retest or skip
< 1.0×Low conviction — likely false breakoutDo not enter Entry 2
Intuition: Why Volume Matters

A fund managing $5 billion cannot hide. When they buy, they move the market. High volume on up days = they're buying aggressively. Low volume on down days = they're not selling, just resting. The volume pattern is the institution's fingerprint on the chart.

Tightness & Priming Patterns
The final signal before entry — when the spring is maximally compressed

Tight Area Definition

// A "tight day" requires BOTH conditions:
daily_range_pct = (High - Low) / Close × 100 ≤ 1.5%
vol_vs_avg = Volume / 50d_avg_volume ≤ 0.5

// A "tight area" = 2+ consecutive tight days
// Best: right side of base, near pivot, decreasing range

Macro Volatility Contraction (Daily)

First30 = mean(daily_range_pct, days 1–30)
Last30 = mean(daily_range_pct, days 31–60)
Contraction = (Last30 / First30 - 1) × 100

// Pass: ≤ −20% (last 30 days measurably quieter)
// Strong: ≤ −35%
// WARNING: A positive value = expanding volatility = base deteriorating

Additional Entry Patterns
Range breakouts, flags, and post-breakout retests

High Tight Flag

The Most Powerful Pattern

Flagpole: 25–50%+ surge in < 10 weeks (often an earnings gap). Flag: 1–4 week tight consolidation < 20% deep on drying volume. Breakout: above flag top on strong volume. Moves from HTF are fast and large.

Post-Breakout Retest (Pullback Entry)

After a valid breakout, the stock pulls back to test the pivot or rising 21 EMA on declining volume. This is often the best risk/reward entry because:

  • ✓
    Stop can be placed just below the pivot — tighter than the original breakout entry
  • ✓
    Volume declining on pullback = no distribution, just digestion
  • ✓
    "Tennis ball" bounce off the level confirms support is real

Entry & Stop Loss System
The complete two-entry framework with independent stops

Entry 1 — Early Entry (Inside the Base)

// Trigger = high of best priming signal
E1_trigger = high of inside bar / reversal / tight setup day
E1_stop = primary support (structural floor of the base)
E1_risk = (E1_trigger - E1_stop) / E1_trigger × 100

// Pass: risk ≤ 4.0%
// Invalid: risk < 0.3% (stale signal, trigger ≈ stop)

Entry 2 — Standard Breakout

// 0.5% above pivot confirms the break is real, not a wick
E2_trigger = pivot × 1.005
E2_stop = pivot × 0.97 (3% back inside base = failed breakout)
E2_risk = (E2_trigger - E2_stop) / E2_trigger × 100 ≈ 3.5%

// Volume gate: only enter E2 if breakout vol ≥ 1.5× 50-day avg

Position Sizing

// Risk a fixed dollar amount per trade
Shares = Risk_$ / (Entry_price - Stop_price)
Position_Value = Shares × Entry_price

// Example: $500 risk, E1 at $235, stop at $227
Shares = 500 / (235 - 227) = 500 / 8 = 62 shares
Position = 62 × 235 = $14,570

Key Intuitions to Memorise
The mental models behind the rules — understand these and the rules make sense
The Coil

A base is a stock being digested by institutions. Each time it pulls back and recovers with less severity, it means fewer sellers remain. The tighter the final contraction, the more compressed the spring. Any new demand hits an empty order book and the price launches.

Why Volume Drying Up = Bullish

If a stock was truly in trouble, sellers would be panicking out. Low volume means they're not — the people who wanted to sell have already sold. The base is quiet because the institution absorbed all the supply. Quiet = accumulation complete.

The RS Line as the Smart Money Detector

Institutions rotate into the best stocks first, before the obvious breakout. If the RS line is rising while price is flat in a base, big money is quietly bidding it higher than the market. When the base resolves, they're already loaded and will push it hard. A leading RS signal is the clearest possible sign of institutional pre-positioning.

Why the Stop Is Structural, Not Percentage-Based

A stop placed at a structurally meaningful level (below a tight area, below primary support, below the base low) means something specific: if that level breaks, the buyers who were defending it have stepped away. The thesis is wrong. A percentage stop (e.g. "3% below entry") is arbitrary — the market doesn't care about your entry price.

Why Weekly Charts for the Base, Daily for Entry

Weekly charts collapse five days of noise into one candle. You see the base shape — the VCP, the volume drying up — without being distracted by random daily wiggles. But entry timing requires the daily chart because priming patterns (inside bars, reversals) last one or two days and are invisible on the weekly.

The Picks and Shovels Rotation

When a theme plays out (e.g. AI), the headline beneficiaries (NVDA, AMD) often run first and then consolidate. The upstream suppliers (chip equipment: LRCX, KLAC, AMAT) benefit from the same capex wave but lag by 3–6 months. Watch for this rotation — when the obvious names are basing, the less obvious upstream names may be setting up for their own run.

The Two Questions Before Any Entry

1. If I'm wrong, where do I get out and how much do I lose? — Define the stop before the entry. The risk must be ≤ 4%.

2. What needs to happen for this trade to work? — The stock must break above the pivot on volume, hold the EMAs, and continue higher. If it can't clear the pivot on volume, the thesis fails.
